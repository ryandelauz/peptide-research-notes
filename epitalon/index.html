Epitalon (AEDG): A Research Overview of the Synthetic Tetrapeptide and the Telomerase Literature

Disclosure: this overview was prepared on behalf of Peptide Titans, a research-peptide supplier, and links once to its product page.

Short answer: Epitalon — also written Epithalon — is the synthetic tetrapeptide Ala-Glu-Asp-Gly, often abbreviated AEDG. It was designed as a short peptide modelled on a bovine pineal extract called epithalamin; the extract and the tetrapeptide are not the same material. The best-known papers, from the early 2000s, report telomerase expression and telomere lengthening in cultured human fibroblasts, and mixed lifespan results in one mouse strain. A 2025 cell-line study from a British group reported telomere lengthening in two normal human cell lines, attributed to telomerase, and a different mechanism in two cancer cell lines. All of that is cell culture or animal work. It is not evidence that the peptide changes human ageing, and it is not an approved medicine.

Identity: a four-residue peptide, two common spellings

The structure is simple: L-alanyl-L-α-glutamyl-L-α-aspartyl-glycine. PubChem lists it as CID 219042, with formula C14H22N4O9 and a computed average mass of 390.35. The usual CAS registry number is 307297-39-8. "Epitalon" and "Epithalon" (and occasionally "Epithalone") are used interchangeably in the literature for this sequence.

A naming caution is worth stating clearly. PubChem's synonym list for CID 219042 also includes "Epithalamin" and "Epithalamine." In the research literature, epithalamin usually refers to a peptide preparation extracted from the bovine pineal gland, not to the synthetic tetrapeptide. Papers that study the extract and papers that study AEDG should not be treated as interchangeable. This article discusses the synthetic tetrapeptide.

The 2003–2004 fibroblast papers

The work that made the peptide widely known is from Vladimir Khavinson's group at the St. Petersburg Institute of Bioregulation and Gerontology.

In June 2003, Khavinson, Bondarev and Butyugov reported in the Bulletin of Experimental Biology and Medicine (PMID 12937682) that adding the peptide to a telomerase-negative human fetal fibroblast culture induced expression of the telomerase catalytic subunit, detectable enzymatic activity, and telomere elongation. The authors interpreted this as reactivation of the telomerase gene in somatic cells. The abstract goes on to extrapolate from this culture result to whole-organism lifespan. A cell-culture experiment does not support that extrapolation.

A follow-up paper in 2004 (PMID 15455129) used primary pulmonary fibroblasts from a 24-week fetus. The untreated cells lost proliferative potential at passage 34, and their mean telomere length was described as appreciably shorter than at passage 10. Addition of the peptide to ageing cultures was reported to lengthen telomeres to a size comparable with early passages. Treated cells then made 10 further divisions (to passage 44) compared with controls and, the authors wrote, continued dividing. They described this as overcoming the Hayflick limit in that culture system.

These are the core cell-culture results that later papers cite. They come from one group, in short reports, and they have been influential well beyond the size of the original datasets. They are a starting point for replication, not a closed case.

A 2025 cell-line study outside the original group

In September 2025, Al-Dulaimi, Thomas, Matta and Saretzki published a paper in Biogerontology (PMID 40908429; PMC12411320) that directly measured telomere length, hTERT expression, telomerase activity and alternative lengthening of telomeres (ALT) after the peptide was added to the cultures. A correction issued in November 2025 replaced incorrect versions of Figures 1–3; the textual findings summarised here are taken from the article text.

The authors treated two normal human lines — IBR.3 fibroblasts and HMEC epithelial cells — daily for three weeks. They reported a significant increase in average telomere length in both lines compared with untreated controls (a four-day exposure of IBR.3 had not been enough). They attributed the effect in these normal lines to upregulation of telomerase. They also treated two telomerase-positive breast-cancer lines, 21NT and BT474, for four days at several concentrations, and reported telomere lengthening there as well. In those cancer lines, however, they reported activation of ALT, assessed by a C-circle assay and by immunofluorescence for PML bodies. They did not observe ALT activation in the normal lines.

Two limits are stated in the paper itself and should travel with any citation. First, this is in vitro work in two-dimensional culture, not an animal or human study. Second, the qPCR telomere assay measures average length across chromosomes and does not show whether every chromosome changes to the same degree. The authors' discussion also extends the findings to people; no human-use or safety conclusion can be drawn from a cell-line experiment, and none is drawn here.

Animal data: what one long mouse study did and did not show

Also in 2003, Anisimov, Khavinson and colleagues reported a lifespan study in female Swiss-derived SHR mice in Biogerontology (PMID 14501183). From three months of age until natural death, 54 mice per group received a monthly five-day course of either saline or the tetrapeptide.

The peptide did not change food consumption, body weight or mean life span. The authors also reported differences in several secondary measures, including estrous function, bone-marrow chromosome aberrations, late-survivor and maximum life span, and leukaemia incidence; total spontaneous tumour incidence was unchanged. These are mixed secondary results in one sex of one outbred mouse stock. They do not show that the peptide extends life or affects cancer risk.

Human evidence: a registered protocol, not a result

There is no FDA-approved indication. A ClinicalTrials.gov search on 12 November 2026 did not identify a completed, peptide-only trial with posted results. One registered record, NCT07888595 (an early-phase, single-group, open-label, multi-agent study listed as enrolling by invitation, with no results), includes this tetrapeptide as one ingredient of a multi-peptide topical formulation used alongside other agents. It is a study design, not an outcome, and a multi-agent design cannot isolate the contribution of any one peptide.

Human studies of the pineal extract epithalamin are sometimes cited in secondary discussions of this peptide. Because the extract and the tetrapeptide are different materials, those extract studies are not summarised here as evidence about AEDG.

Handling and documentation in the lab

The peptide is small and polar, and the expected monoisotopic or average mass is a direct check on identity. General peptide-handling practice applies. Bachem's published notes flag the hygroscopic character of peptides and recommend bringing a sealed vial to ambient temperature in a dry atmosphere before opening, then resealing promptly; they also advise aliquoting solutions and avoiding long-term storage in solution. The Peptide Titans listing specifies −20 °C to −80 °C for long-term storage of the sealed lyophilised vial, protected from light and moisture.

A useful certificate should report an identity result against the expected mass of Ala-Glu-Asp-Gly, a stated purity method, and a lot number that matches the vial. With any supplier, the lot certificate is the document to rely on. Results from one lot should not be generalised to another.

Bottom line

Epitalon is a four-residue synthetic peptide with a clear structure and a literature that is wider in reputation than in independent data. The original fibroblast papers report telomerase expression, telomere lengthening and extra divisions in culture. A 2025 cell-line study adds quantitative measurements in two normal lines and raises a distinct ALT finding in two cancer lines, which is a reason for careful experimental design, not a human conclusion. A long mouse study did not move mean lifespan. None of this establishes a use in people. If you work with the peptide, specify AEDG, confirm the mass, record the lot, and keep claims inside the system you actually measured.
